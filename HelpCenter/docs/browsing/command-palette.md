# Use the Command Bar

The Command Bar is where you type. It opens sites, searches the web, finds your open tabs, saved tabs and history, switches Spaces, opens settings and runs commands. In Settings it's called the command palette.

## Open the Command Bar {#open-command-bar}

1. Click New Tab in the sidebar, or press Command-T.
2. Type an address, a search, or the name of a tab, setting or command.
3. Press Return.

Shortcut: **Command-T**

## Ask Ode how to do something {#ask-ode-help}

1. Open the Command Bar, type help or ode, and press Tab. Or type ? and a space.
2. Ask your question, such as how do I split view.
3. Press Return to run Do It, or press Tab first to read the guide instead.

You can also type a question straight into the Command Bar. When one answer fits, it shows first.

An answer shows its steps, the keys that do the same thing, Do It, which does it for you, and Read More, which opens the guide. Tab moves between the two buttons and Escape closes the Command Bar. Answers come from these guides, and you can turn them off with Answers in Settings > Search.

## Change the address of the current tab {#edit-current-address}

1. Click the address at the top of the sidebar, or press Command-L.
2. Type a new address or search.
3. Press Return to open it in the same tab.

Shortcut: **Command-L**

In a Quick Window, the Command Bar opens from the Quick Window's address: the address stays where it was, ready to edit, and the results show below it. What you choose opens in the Quick Window, and a tab opens in the browser window.

## Read the results

The first row is what Return does. When one result clearly fits what you typed, it leads as the Top Hit: the address you're typing, a page you visit often, or the result you picked before for the same words. Otherwise the first row searches for what you typed.

Below it come your open tabs, pinned and saved tabs, history, actions and other Spaces, then search suggestions. Right under the search, one short answer can show: a conversion, the time somewhere, a definition, or, with Web Answers on, a Wikipedia summary. It never takes the first row, so Return still does what the first row says. Before you type anything, the Command Bar shows your recent tabs and a few actions.

As you type the start of a site you've visited, the rest of its address appears in gray. Press Return to go there, or Tab or Right Arrow to accept it and keep typing.

## Search the web {#search-the-web}

1. Open the Command Bar.
2. Type what you're looking for.
3. Press Return. Ode searches with the search engine your Space uses.

Shortcut: **Command-T**

## Go straight to the top result of a search {#open-top-result}

1. Type a search in the Command Bar.
2. Press Shift-Return. The tab goes straight to the search's first result instead of the results page.
3. On a site, tab or history row, Shift-Return still opens it in Split View. The selected row says which.

Shortcut: **Shift-Return**

Ode finds the result with DuckDuckGo, or Kagi when it's your Space's search engine, and sends your search only when you press Shift-Return. It works in a Quick Window too. Offline, Shift-Return opens the results as Return does.

## Search YouTube, Wikipedia or another site from the Command Bar {#search-a-site}

1. Open the Command Bar and type a site's shortcut, such as yt for YouTube or w for Wikipedia.
2. Press Tab. The site's name appears beside the field.
3. Type your search and press Return.

Shortcut: **Tab**

You can also type $ and the shortcut, then a space, such as $yt.

Tab to search providers and Space to search providers in Settings > Search set how you pick a site. Turn off Tab to search providers and Tab no longer picks one. Turn on Space to search providers and a shortcut and a space, such as yt and a space, picks it too.

## Ask ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity from the Command Bar {#ask-ai-from-command-bar}

1. Open the Command Bar and type the assistant's name, such as chatgpt, claude or perplexity.
2. Press Tab.
3. Type your question and press Return. It opens on the assistant's own site.

Shortcut: **Tab**

Ode only opens the assistant's website. It sends nothing to it by itself.

## Find an open tab {#find-open-tab}

1. Open the Command Bar.
2. Type part of the tab's title or address. Open tabs show first.
3. Press Return to switch to it. Type @tabs and a space to see only tabs.

Shortcut: **Command-T**

## Search only tabs, history, saved tabs, actions or Spaces {#narrow-command-bar-results}

1. Open the Command Bar.
2. Type @tabs, @history, @saved, @actions or @spaces, then a space or Tab.
3. Type your search. Press Escape to leave the filter and keep your text.

## Run a command or open a setting by typing its name {#run-command-by-name}

1. Open the Command Bar.
2. Type what you want to do, such as archive, downloads, split or reader. Settings pages show too.
3. Press Return to run it. Initials work as well, such as tsv for Toggle Split View.

Shortcut: **Command-T**

## Do quick math in the Command Bar {#command-bar-calculator}

1. Open the Command Bar.
2. Type a sum, such as 24 * 7. The answer shows below the search.
3. Select the answer and press Return to copy it.

## Convert units and check the time somewhere else {#command-bar-conversions}

1. Type a conversion, such as 5 km in miles or how many ounces in a pound.
2. Or type time in and a place, such as time in Tokyo, or a time to convert, such as 3pm New York in London.
3. The answer shows below the search. Press Down, then Return to copy it.

Conversions and times are worked out on your Mac, so nothing is sent. Turn them off with Conversions under Also show in Settings > Search.

## Look up a word in the dictionary {#command-bar-dictionary}

1. Type define and the word, such as define ephemeral, or just the word.
2. The Mac's definition shows below the search. Press Down, then Return to open it in Dictionary.

The definition comes from your Mac, so nothing is sent. A Mac that has never used its dictionary downloads it first, and definitions show once it's ready.

## Show Wikipedia summaries in the Command Bar {#web-answers}

1. Choose Ode > Settings, then click Search.
2. Under Also show, turn on Web Answers.
3. Type a topic's exact name, such as Albert Einstein. A summary shows below the search, and Return opens the article.

It's off until you turn it on. Then what you type goes to Wikipedia, never in a private window. Pictures show only when they're freely licensed, with their author and licence.

## Open a link you just copied {#paste-and-go}

1. Choose Ode > Settings, click Search, and turn on Paste and Go under Also show.
2. Copy a link in any app, then open the Command Bar. The link shows as the first result.
3. Press Return to open it.

Paste and Go is off until you turn it on.

## Open a Command Bar result in a new tab, Split View or Quick Window {#open-result-elsewhere}

1. Select a result in the Command Bar.
2. Hold Command for a background tab, Shift-Command for a new tab, Shift for Split View, or Option for a Quick Window.
3. Press Return while you hold the keys.

On a search, Shift opens the search's first result instead of Split View.

## Keys in the Command Bar

- Up and Down arrows move through the results. Option-Up and Option-Down jump between sections.
- Tab picks the site whose shortcut you typed, accepts the gray address, or moves to the next result.
- Return runs the selected result.
- Shift-Return on a search opens its first result. On a site, tab or history row it opens Split View. The selected row says which.
- Shift-Delete removes the selected history result, or makes the Command Bar forget a result it learned.
- Escape leaves a site search or filter, then clears the gray address, then closes the Command Bar.

## Search settings

## Change your default search engine {#change-default-search}

1. Choose Ode > Settings, then click Search.
2. Under Default search, pick a search engine or an AI assistant.
3. A Space can use its own engine: pick it under Search with on the Space's Browsing page.

## Add your own search engine or site search {#add-search-provider}

1. Choose Ode > Settings, then click Search.
2. Click Add Provider…, then enter a name, a shortcut and the search address with %s where the search words go.
3. Click Save. Type the shortcut in the Command Bar and press Tab to use it.

Search addresses must use HTTPS.

## Change or turn off a site search shortcut {#change-search-shortcut}

1. Choose Ode > Settings, then click Search.
2. Click a provider to change its shortcuts and options, or turn it off.
3. To undo your changes, click Restore Defaults. Providers you added stay.

## Hide AI answers in Google and other search results {#hide-ai-overviews}

1. Choose Ode > Settings, then click Search.
2. Click your search engine in the list of providers.
3. Turn on its option, such as Hide AI Overviews for Google.

## Turn off search suggestions {#search-suggestions-off}

1. Choose Ode > Settings, then click Search.
2. Turn off Search suggestions under Default search.
3. A Space with its own search engine has its own switch on its Browsing page.

Suggestions send what you type to the search engine. Private windows never fetch them, and Ode never sends an address you typed, text that looks like a password, key or card number, or text a tab or your history already answers.

## Change what the Command Bar shows {#command-bar-results-settings}

1. Choose Ode > Settings, then click Search.
2. Under Results, choose Blended or sections, Top Hit and Show match reasons.
3. Under Sections, turn kinds of results on or off and drag them into the order you want.

## Make the Command Bar forget what it learned {#clear-learned-choices}

1. To forget one result, select it in the Command Bar and press Shift-Delete.
2. To forget everything, choose Ode > Settings, click Search, then click Clear Learned Choices.
3. To stop learning, turn off Learn from my choices.

What the Command Bar learns stays on this Mac and is never learned in a private window. It learns for each Space, or each Sub-Space group.

Search providers, their shortcuts and what the Command Bar learns stay on this Mac.
